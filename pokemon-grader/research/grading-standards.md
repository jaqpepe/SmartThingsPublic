# Pokémon TCG Card Grading Standards Research

*Research compiled for PokéGrader AI — Phase 1*

## 1. PSA Grading Standards

### 1.1 PSA Numeric Scale (Full)

PSA (Professional Sports Authenticator) uses a 1–10 numeric scale. Half-point grades are not issued.

| Grade | Label | Key Criteria |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| PSA 10 | Gem Mint | Perfect or near-perfect in all areas |
| PSA 9 | Mint | One minor allowable flaw |
| PSA 8 | NM-MT (Near Mint–Mint) | Minor corner, surface, or centering flaws |
| PSA 7 | Near Mint | One or two notable flaws |
| PSA 6 | EX-MT (Excellent–Mint) | Several minor flaws |
| PSA 5 | Excellent | Moderate wear throughout |
| PSA 4 | VG-EX (Very Good–Excellent) | Obvious wear, possible creases |
| PSA 3 | Very Good | Moderate rounding, light creasing |
| PSA 2 | Good | Heavy wear, creases, staining |
| PSA 1 | Poor | Extensive damage |

---

### 1.2 Detailed Grade Definitions

#### PSA 10 — Gem Mint
- **Centering**: Front 55/45 or better (left-right AND top-bottom). Back 75/25 or better.
- **Corners**: All four corners must be perfectly sharp with no fraying or fuzzing whatsoever.
- **Surfaces**: No scratches, print lines, stains, or loss of gloss. Holo surface must be free of any scratches.
- **Edges**: All four edges must be perfectly clean with no roughness, nicks, or chipping.
- **Other**: Print must be crisp and perfectly registered. Card must be free of manufacturing defects that detract from appearance.
- **Notes**: PSA 10s are extremely rare. Even factory-fresh cards often fail due to print centering or surface micro-scratches.

#### PSA 9 — Mint
- **Centering**: Front 60/40 or better. Back 75/25 or better.
- **Corners**: Four sharp corners; one minor imperfection allowable (barely perceptible under 5× magnification).
- **Surfaces**: Minor surface wear allowable. Slight print imperfections may be present.
- **Edges**: Minimal roughness allowable; edges are essentially clean.
- **Other**: Card may have one very slight wax stain on reverse or minor print imperfection.

#### PSA 8 — Near Mint–Mint
- **Centering**: Front 65/35 or better. Back 80/20 or better.
- **Corners**: Slight wear on one or two corners; no heavy fraying.
- **Surfaces**: Light surface scratches visible under inspection; print lines may be present.
- **Edges**: Slight roughness on edges acceptable.

#### PSA 7 — Near Mint
- **Centering**: Front 70/30 or better. Back 85/15 or better.
- **Corners**: One to three corners showing slight wear or fuzzing.
- **Surfaces**: Moderate surface wear; light scratches visible without magnification.
- **Edges**: Moderate roughness acceptable.

#### PSA 6 — Excellent–Mint
- **Centering**: Front 75/25 or better. Back 90/10 or better.
- **Corners**: Slight rounding on two or more corners.
- **Surfaces**: Some surface wear; scratches visible to naked eye.
- **Edges**: Roughness present.

#### PSA 5 — Excellent
- **Centering**: Front 80/20 or better.
- **Corners**: Moderate rounding of corners. Slight whitening may be present.
- **Surfaces**: Light play wear visible throughout. Minor creasing possible.
- **Edges**: Some roughness and possible minor nicks.

#### PSA 4 — Very Good–Excellent
- **Centering**: Front heavily off-center acceptable.
- **Corners**: Obvious rounding and fraying. Whitening on corners.
- **Surfaces**: Heavy surface wear. Scratches visible.
- **Edges**: Roughness and nicks.

#### PSA 3 — Very Good
- **Corners**: Moderate-to-heavy rounding with some layering.
- **Surfaces**: Light creasing. Some staining possible.
- **Edges**: Heavy roughness.

#### PSA 2 — Good
- **Corners**: Severely rounded; some layering at corners.
- **Surfaces**: Creases, stains present. Heavy scratches.
- **Edges**: Severe roughness.

#### PSA 1 — Poor
- **Description**: Card heavily played, extremely worn. Soiling, staining, writing, and/or major damage throughout. Card structurally intact but heavily damaged.

### 1.3 Centering Tolerances (Critical Detail)

PSA measures centering as a ratio of larger border to smaller border.

#### Front of Card:
| PSA Grade | Left/Right Max | Top/Bottom Max |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| PSA 10    | 55/45          | 55/45          |
| PSA 9     | 60/40          | 60/40          |
| PSA 8     | 65/35          | 65/35          |
| PSA 7     | 70/30          | 70/30          |
| PSA 6     | 75/25          | 75/25          |

#### Back of Card:
| PSA Grade | Left/Right Max |
|-----------|----------------|
| PSA 10    | 75/25          |
| PSA 9     | 75/25          |
| PSA 8     | 80/20          |
| PSA 7     | 85/15          |
| PSA 6     | 90/10          |

**How to calculate**: Measure the pixel width of each border (left, right, top, bottom).
Centering ratio = (larger border width) / (total of both opposite borders) × 100.
Example: Left border 110px, Right border 90px → ratio = 110/(110+90) = 55% → "55/45"

### 1.4 Surface Scratch Categories

**Light Scratches:**
- Barely visible under direct light
- No disruption to card gloss
- On holo cards: fine swirl marks visible only at specific angles
- Do not depress below surface plane

**Moderate Scratches:**
- Visible to naked eye under normal lighting
- May catch light at multiple angles
- Some disruption to holo pattern
- May show slight depth

**Heavy Scratches:**
- Clearly visible without close inspection
- Significant disruption to gloss/holo
- Noticeable depth or cross-hatching
- Card clearly played or damaged

---

### 1.5 Corner Wear Stages

From best to worst:

1. **Gem Sharp / Perfect Sharp**: Perfectly pointed corner, no fraying whatsoever under 10× magnification.
2. **Slight Fuzzing**: Very minimal fraying at the very tip, requires magnification to see.
3. **Fuzzing / Fuzzy**: Visible fraying or fuzzing at corner tip without magnification.
4. **Slight Rounding**: Corner tip beginning to show physical rounding.
5. **Rounding**: Clear rounding of corner; tip no longer sharp.
6. **Heavy Rounding**: Severe rounding; may show layering or whitening.
7. **Heavy Wear**: Corner severely damaged; layering, peeling, or whitening.

---

### 1.6 Edge Wear Definitions

- **Clean**: Perfectly smooth, no roughness or imperfections.
- **Slight Roughness**: Minor texture change along edge; not visually distracting.
- **Moderate Roughness**: Texture clearly different from clean edge under close inspection.
- **Heavy Roughness**: Significant texture, possible chipping.
- **Whitening**: White paper core showing through dark edges (common on dark-bordered cards).
- **Nicks**: Small chips or cuts in the edge.
- **Chipping**: Larger portions of edge material missing.

---

### 1.7 Print Defects — Affects Grade vs. Does Not

#### Defects That Affect Grade:
- Print lines (repeating lines from printing process, visible on card surface)
- Color bleeding/smearing
- Misregistration (colors out of alignment)
- Missing ink spots
- Staining from manufacturing process
- Heavy dot patterns that disrupt image clarity

#### Defects That Do NOT Affect Grade (PSA Policy):
- Minor print imperfections inherent to the manufacturing process that do not detract significantly
- Factory error cards (misprints) that still grade well on physical condition
- Texture variations within the holo pattern
- Normal variation in card stock

**Note**: PSA distinguishes between *post-production* damage and *factory* defects. Factory defects may still allow higher grades if physical condition is excellent.

---

### 1.8 Holo Scratches — PSA Specific Treatment

Holo scratches are among the most common reasons a card fails to achieve PSA 10.

- **Detection**: Holo scratches are often only visible under raking/directional light at specific angles.
- **Types**:
  - *Swirl scratches*: Circular patterns from cleaning or storage
  - *Directional scratches*: Linear marks from sliding in/out of sleeves
  - *Press marks*: Indentations that break the holo surface
- **PSA 10 threshold**: Zero visible holo scratches under any reasonable lighting condition.
- **PSA 9**: One or two light holo scratches may be allowable if extremely minor.
- **Photography note**: Standard photography often cannot capture holo scratches. Raking light at 45° to card surface is required.

---

### 1.9 PSA Population Report

The PSA Population Report ("pop report") tracks:
- Total number of copies graded at each grade level
- "PSA 10 Population" = number of PSA 10s of that specific card
- "PSA 10 Pop %": percentage of all graded copies that achieved PSA 10
- Used to estimate card rarity and grade difficulty
- Cards with low pop counts at high grades are generally more valuable

## 2. TAG Grading Standards

### 2.1 TAG Numeric Scale

TAG (Technical Authentication & Grading) uses a more granular scale with half-points:

| Grade | Label |
|-------|-------|
| 10    | Gem Mint |
| 9.5   | (Near Gem Mint) |
| 9     | Mint |
| 8.5   | (Near Mint-Mint+) |
| 8     | Near Mint-Mint |
| 7     | Near Mint |
| 6     | Excellent-Mint |
| 5     | Excellent |
| 4     | Very Good-Excellent |
| 3     | Very Good |
| 2     | Good |
| 1     | Poor |

---

### 2.2 TAG Sub-Grade System

TAG's defining innovation is its four sub-category scores, each graded independently:

1. **Centering** — Border width ratios, front and back
2. **Surface** — Scratches, print defects, stains, loss of gloss
3. **Edges** — Roughness, nicks, whitening on all four edges
4. **Corners** — All four corners, assessed individually

Each sub-grade is assigned on the same 1–10 scale (with half-points).

---

### 2.3 TAG Composite Score Calculation

**Weighted Average Formula:**

```
Composite = (Corners × 0.35) + (Surfaces × 0.35) + (Centering × 0.20) + (Edges × 0.10)
```

Weights:
- **Corners**: 35% (heaviest weight — most visible grade indicator)
- **Surfaces**: 35% (equally weighted — holo and surface condition)
- **Centering**: 20%
- **Edges**: 10% (lightest — edge damage less impactful than corners/surface)

The composite score is then rounded to the nearest half-point (e.g., 8.73 → 8.5, 9.25 → 9.5).

---

### 2.4 TAG Centering Methodology

TAG measures centering using physical measurement tools:
- Borders measured in millimeters using calibrated measurement tools
- Reports as a ratio (e.g., "52/48" meaning the larger side has 52% of total border width)
- Both left/right AND top/bottom measured for front
- Back measured as well (though typically more lenient)

**TAG Centering Score Guide:**
| Ratio       | Score |
|-------------|-------|
| 50/50–51/49 | 10    |
| 52/48–53/47 | 9.5   |
| 54/46–55/45 | 9     |
| 56/44–57/43 | 8.5   |
| 58/42–60/40 | 8     |
| 61/39–63/37 | 7     |
| 64/36–65/35 | 6     |
| 66/34–70/30 | 5     |
| 71/29–75/25 | 4     |
| 76/24+      | 3     |

---

### 2.5 TAG Report Card Format

TAG produces a physical "Report Card" label on the slab, featuring:
- Card name, set, number, language
- Large central composite grade (most prominent feature)
- Four sub-grade boxes arranged in a 2×2 grid:
  ```
  [Centering] [Surfaces]
  [Edges]     [Corners]
  ```
- Each sub-grade shows the numeric score
- Color coding on sub-grades (green = high, yellow = medium, red = low)
- Certification number and barcode
- Small card thumbnail image on some slabs

---

### 2.6 TAG vs. PSA Philosophy

| Aspect | PSA | TAG |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Scale | Integers only (1–10) | Half-points (1, 1.5... 9.5, 10) |
| Sub-grades | No | Yes — 4 sub-categories |
| Centering | Front + back simple check | Precise mm measurement |
| Report | Simple label | Full report card with sub-grades |
| Factory defects | Generally penalized | Distinguished from post-production |
| Philosophy | Holistic assessment | Technical/analytical breakdown |
| Holo scratches | Strict — any visible = -1 grade | Scored per severity |

**PSA philosophy**: Holistic, gestalt assessment. A card is what it is overall.

**TAG philosophy**: Technical breakdown. Each attribute scored independently, combined via weighted formula. More transparent about what limits the grade.

---

### 2.7 Factory Defects vs. Post-Production Damage

**Factory Defects** (from manufacturing):
- Print lines, dot patterns, color variations
- Minor centering issues from the press
- Texture variations
- TAG distinguishes these and may not penalize as heavily as post-production damage

**Post-Production Damage** (from handling/storage):
- Corner wear from touching, handling
- Surface scratches from sleeves or storage
- Edge roughness from shuffling
- Stains from liquids or contact
- TAG penalizes these more heavily as they represent preventable damage

---

### 2.8 TAG Turnaround Tiers

TAG offers multiple service tiers (pricing and speed vary):
- **Economy**: Longest turnaround, lowest price per card
- **Standard**: Standard turnaround
- **Express**: Faster turnaround, higher price
- **Premium/Walkthrough**: Same-day or next-day for high-value cards

Cards with declared values above certain thresholds automatically qualify for higher service levels with additional insurance.

## 3. Technical Measurement Standards

### 3.1 Centering Calculation from Pixel Widths

**Method:**
1. Take a high-resolution scan or photo of the card, perfectly flat and perpendicular
2. Identify the border edges (where the card artwork/border begins vs. the white card edge)
3. Measure pixel widths: Left (L), Right (R), Top (T), Bottom (B)
4. Calculate: Left-Right ratio = max(L,R) / (L+R) × 100
5. Calculate: Top-Bottom ratio = max(T,B) / (T+B) × 100
6. Express as "larger/smaller" (e.g., 55/45)

**Example:**
- Left border: 85px, Right border: 115px
- L+R total = 200px
- Right is larger: 115/200 = 57.5%
- Centering = "58/42" (rounded to nearest integer)

**Accuracy notes:**
- Perspective distortion from camera angle introduces error
- Consistent flat scanning (1200 DPI flatbed scanner) gives most accurate results
- Phone camera photos at even slight angles can skew measurements by 3–5%
- AI estimation from photos is ±3% accuracy at best

---

### 3.2 Corner Grading Photography

**Standard photography setup for corner grading:**
- **Macro lens**: 1:1 or better reproduction ratio
- **Lighting**: Two soft lights at 45° angles from opposite sides
- **Angle**: Perpendicular to card surface (top-down)
- **Resolution**: Minimum 1200 DPI scan or equivalent photo
- **Depth of field**: All four corners must be in sharp focus

**Corner grading under magnification:**
- 5× magnification for PSA standards
- 10× magnification for TAG technical analysis
- Fiber optic "raking light" at 10–15° to surface reveals texture and fuzzing

---

### 3.3 Common Defect Types

| Defect | Description | Visible How |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Whitening | White paper core showing at corners/edges | Direct light, dark-bordered cards |
| Creases | Fold damage to card stock | Raking light (side lighting) |
| Stains | Discoloration from liquids, oils, etc. | Direct light |
| Print lines | Repeating lines from printing rollers | Direct/diffuse light |
| Holo scratches | Fine marks on holographic surface | Raking directional light at 45° |
| Surface scratches | Non-holo surface marks | Raking light |
| Indentations | Dents without breaking surface | Raking light |
| Print smear | Ink spreading beyond intended area | Direct light |
| Ink missing | Spots where ink failed to apply | Direct light |
| Color fade | Lightened areas from sun/age | Comparison to reference |

---

### 3.4 Lighting and Defect Visibility

**Direct (flat) lighting:**
- Best for: Stains, print defects, color fades, major scratches
- Misses: Holo scratches, light surface texture changes, subtle indentations

**Raking (side/angled) lighting:**
- Best for: Holo scratches, indentations, surface texture, subtle corner fuzzing
- Light at 10°–45° from card surface at various angles
- Essential for complete defect assessment

**UV lighting:**
- Reveals: Stains not visible under normal light, restoration attempts, whitening
- Used by advanced graders to detect hidden damage

**Recommendation for photos:**
- Minimum two photos: one under diffuse light, one under raking light
- For holo cards: third photo with directional light specifically for holo surface

---

### 3.5 Image Resolution Requirements

**For accurate visual analysis:**

| Use Case | Minimum Resolution |
|----------|-------------------|
| General grading | 600 DPI equivalent |
| Corner assessment | 1200 DPI equivalent |
| Holo scratch detection | 2400 DPI + raking light |
| AI analysis (Claude) | Full phone camera resolution (12MP+) |
| PSA/TAG submission photo | 300 DPI at full card size |

**Practical guidance:**
- Modern smartphone cameras (12+ megapixels) are sufficient for general analysis
- Macro lens attachment significantly improves corner assessment
- Flatbed scanner (Epson Perfection V600 or similar) is gold standard
- Images should be at least 1500 × 2100 pixels for a standard Pokémon card (2.5" × 3.5")

**AI Analysis note:**
- Claude Vision operates best with clear, well-lit, high-contrast images
- Blurry or low-resolution images will result in "Unable to assess" responses
- JPEG compression at high quality (>85%) is acceptable; lower quality degrades analysis

- Market value at same "equivalent" grade may differ between graders

## 5. Pokémon-Specific Grading Notes

### Card Stock Eras
- **Wizards of the Coast era (1999–2003)**: Thicker cardstock, more susceptible to whitening
- **Nintendo/PUI era (2003–2010)**: Different cardstock, different wear patterns
- **Modern era (2010+)**: Thinner stock, holo patterns are different

### Holo Pattern Types
- **Classic holo**: Star pattern on artwork holo
- **Cosmos holo**: Swirling pattern (ex, later sets)
- **Reverse holo**: Holo on non-holo cards; common in modern sets
- **Full Art/Special Art**: Different holo characteristics

### Common Grade Killers (Pokémon-specific)
1. **Holo scratches** — Most common PSA 10 killer for vintage holos
2. **Centering** — Print run centering issues are common in WotC era
3. **Corner whitening** — Dark-bordered cards (Base Set, Jungle, Fossil) show whitening easily
4. **Print lines** — Common in certain print runs
5. **Card back staining** — Blue card backs show staining easily
